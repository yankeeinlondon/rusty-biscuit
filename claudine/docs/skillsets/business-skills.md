# Agent Skills for Company Definition & Competitive Analysis

**What are agent skills?**  
Agent skills refer to the specialized capabilities an analyst or AI agent needs to gather, interpret and synthesize information about companies.  
For defining a company, skills revolve around understanding its structure, products, business model, stakeholders and financial health.  
For competitive analysis, skills focus on evaluating the external environment, competitors, market forces and strategic positioning.

## Explanation of skills

### Company summarization

This foundational skill involves collecting accurate and up‑to‑date information about a company. Analysts review official websites, annual reports and press releases to capture facts such as founding date, headquarters, mission, ownership and leadership. Clear summaries provide context for deeper analysis.

### Business model analysis

Business models explain how a company creates, delivers and captures value. Analysts map the firm’s value proposition, customer segments, revenue streams and cost structure using tools such as the business model canvas. Understanding the model reveals how the company generates profit and where risks or opportunities lie.

### Product & service portfolio

Evaluating a firm’s portfolio requires listing major products and services and classifying them by growth and market share. The Boston Consulting Group (BCG) matrix is a common tool. It divides offerings into **stars**, **cash cows**, **question marks** and **dogs** based on their relative market share and market growth [oai_citation:9‡corporatefinanceinstitute.com](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/management/boston-consulting-group-bcg-matrix/#:~:text=The%20Boston%20Consulting%20Group%20Matrix,in%20CFI%E2%80%99s%20Business%20Strategy%20Course). This analysis helps prioritize investment in high‑growth or profitable products and consider divesting low‑growth, low‑share offerings.

### Market segmentation & targeting

Market segmentation separates potential customers into smaller groups based on shared characteristics such as demographics, behaviors, geography or psychographics [oai_citation:10‡investopedia.com](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/marketsegmentation.asp#:~:text=Market%20segmentation%20separates%20a%20large,groups%20for%20more%20focused%20marketing). Accurate segmentation allows targeted marketing, tailored products and improved customer engagement. Analysts use research reports, surveys and consumer data to define segments and evaluate which segments the company targets effectively.

### Financial analysis

To understand a company’s health, analysts examine revenue, profit margins, cash flow, debt levels and growth rates. This includes comparing financial ratios to industry benchmarks and assessing trends over time. Reliable data come from audited financial statements, SEC filings and financial databases.

### Stakeholder & governance insights

Corporate governance analysis involves identifying key shareholders, board members and management and evaluating governance practices. Proxy statements reveal board composition, executive compensation and shareholder voting results. Analysts may also consider activism, family ownership or institutional investors that influence strategy.

### Regulatory & legal environment

Industry regulations and legal risks can materially affect a company’s operations. Analysts research relevant laws, licensing requirements, pending legislation and litigation. Government databases and legal news help identify compliance burdens or opportunities arising from regulation.

### ESG/CSR factors

Environmental, social and governance (ESG) factors influence investor perceptions and can indicate long‑term sustainability. Analysts review CSR reports, ESG ratings and sustainability initiatives to evaluate how the company manages environmental impact, labor practices and corporate governance.

### SWOT analysis

A **SWOT analysis** is a strategic technique used to identify a company’s **strengths**, **weaknesses**, **opportunities** and **threats** [oai_citation:11‡investopedia.com](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/s/swot.asp#:~:text=A%20SWOT%20analysis%20is%20a,strengths%2C%20weaknesses%2C%20opportunities%2C%20and%20threats). Strengths are internal advantages like proprietary technology; weaknesses are internal limitations such as high debt. Opportunities and threats arise from external factors like regulatory changes or new competitors. SWOT helps summarize how well a company is positioned in the market and informs strategic planning.

### PESTEL analysis

A **PESTEL analysis** evaluates the macro‑environmental context—political, economic, social, technological, environmental and legal factors—that can affect the business [oai_citation:12‡corporatefinanceinstitute.com](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/management/pestel-analysis/#:~:text=What%20is%20a%20PESTEL%20Analysis%3F). Political factors include taxation and trade policies, economic factors involve interest rates and inflation, social factors encompass demographic and lifestyle trends, technological factors cover innovation and infrastructure, environmental factors relate to climate and sustainability, and legal factors include regulations and intellectual property laws. This holistic scan helps anticipate external opportunities and risks.

### Porter’s five forces

Michael Porter’s **five forces** framework assesses the competitive forces shaping an industry: the **threat of new entrants**, **bargaining power of suppliers**, **bargaining power of buyers**, **threat of substitute products** and **rivalry among existing competitors** [oai_citation:13‡isc.hbs.edu](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx#:~:text=The%20Five%20Forces%20is%20a,is%20divided%20among%20industry%20actors). The model helps determine industry attractiveness and guides strategy. For example, high supplier power can squeeze margins, while low entry barriers can attract new competitors.

### Benchmarking

**Benchmarking** compares a company’s performance, processes or products with those of competitors or industry leaders to identify improvement opportunities. Business News Daily notes that benchmarking means measuring quality, performance and growth by analyzing the processes and procedures of others [oai_citation:14‡businessnewsdaily.com](https://www.businessnewsdaily.com/15960-benchmarking-benefits-small-business.html#:~:text=While%20every%20business%20is%20unique,demands%20of%20the%20modern%20market). Companies use internal, competitive and strategic benchmarking to improve efficiency, adopt best practices and close performance gaps.

### Competitor identification & positioning

Identifying competitors involves listing direct, indirect and potential entrants. The U.S. Small Business Administration explains that a competitive analysis should identify competitors by product line or market segment and assess their market share, strengths, weaknesses and barriers to entry [oai_citation:15‡sba.gov](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis#:~:text=Competitive%20analysis%20helps%20you%20learn,edge%20that%20creates%20sustainable%20revenue). Analysts also consider each competitor’s positioning, target markets and differentiation strategies.

### Pricing & revenue models

Analyzing pricing means reviewing how the company and its competitors price their products or services and how those prices relate to value. Investopedia notes that **competitive pricing** involves setting prices strategically relative to competitors and may include pricing above, at or below the competition [oai_citation:16‡investopedia.com](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/c/competitive-pricing.asp#:~:text=What%20Is%20Competitive%20Pricing%3F). Understanding pricing strategies helps gauge revenue potential and detect aggressive discounting or premium positioning.

### Sentiment & reputation monitoring

**Sentiment analysis** (also called opinion mining) uses natural language processing to determine whether the tone of text is positive, negative or neutral [oai_citation:17‡techtarget.com](https://www.techtarget.com/searchbusinessanalytics/definition/opinion-mining-sentiment-mining#:~:text=Sentiment%20analysis%2C%20also%20referred%20to,positive%2C%20negative%20or%20neutral%20feelings). Analysts track sentiment in social media, news articles, product reviews and surveys to gauge public perception. Monitoring sentiment can reveal reputational risks or highlight strong brand loyalty.

### Trend & innovation scanning

Staying ahead of industry trends and innovation involves tracking emerging technologies, patents, R&D spending and market disruptions. Analysts monitor news, academic publications, patent filings and venture capital investments to identify new entrants or technologies that could reshape the competitive landscape.
